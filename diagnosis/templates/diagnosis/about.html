About the Project

What is PashuRakshak?

PashuRakshak (पशुरक्षक) is a beginner-friendly prototype livestock health screening platform designed to help farmers, paravets, and cattle owners identify potential health risks from observed symptoms at an early stage.

Early detection of infectious diseases such as Foot and Mouth Disease (FMD), Lumpy Skin Disease, and Hemorrhagic Septicemia is vital to preventing devastating herd mortality and economic losses. PashuRakshak provides rapid preliminary screening guidance to help farmers recognize when urgent veterinary attention is required.

How PashuRakshak Works

1
Farmer Observes & Enters Signs

Selects species (cow, buffalo, goat, etc.) and checks observed symptoms like fever, skin nodules, or salivation.

2
Django Backend Scoring

A transparent weighted symptom matching engine compares inputs against the livestock disease knowledge base.

3
Risk & Veterinary Guidance

Computes confidence, assigns risk level (LOW to CRITICAL), and issues actionable next steps for veterinary consultation.

Farmer enters symptoms ➔ Django backend ➔ Symptom analysis ➔ Disease matching ➔ Risk assessment ➔ Preliminary result ➔ Veterinary consultation
Disease Knowledge Base

Currently includes curated profiles for 10 major livestock diseases:

  • Foot and Mouth Disease (FMD)
  • Lumpy Skin Disease
  • Hemorrhagic Septicemia (HS)
  • Peste des Petits Ruminants (PPR)
  • Anthrax
  • Mastitis
  • Black Quarter (BQ)
  • Sheep and Goat Pox
  • Classical Swine Fever
  • Avian Influenza (Bird Flu)
Safety & Medical Protocol

PashuRakshak strictly observes ethical veterinary safety rules:

  • Uses non-definitive phrasing: "Possible condition" and "Potential risk".
  • Never prescribes medication or specifies antibiotic dosages.
  • Immediately flags critical emergency conditions (Anthrax, Bird Flu, Septicemia) with urgent alerts.
Prototype Notice & Disclaimer

This screening tool is intended solely for educational, preliminary triage, and research purposes. It cannot replace clinical examination, laboratory culture, PCR testing, or expert diagnosis by a licensed veterinarian.
